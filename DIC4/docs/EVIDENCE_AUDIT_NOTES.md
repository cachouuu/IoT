## Architecture and implementation facts

The documented prototype is a single intersection with NS/EW straight-through demand, an ARM numerical controller, AXI command/readback, and an FPGA phase controller. The archived experiment report identifies `xc7z020`; no complete platform description, bitstream, or current device access is supplied here. Avoid substituting an Artix-only platform claim from unrelated memory.

The candidate plan IDs are separate from policy IDs:

| Plan ID | NS green | NS yellow | All red | EW green | EW yellow | All red | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 12 s | 2 s | 1 s | 12 s | 2 s | 1 s | 30 s |
| 1 | 16 s | 2 s | 1 s | 8 s | 2 s | 1 s | 30 s |
| 2 | 8 s | 2 s | 1 s | 16 s | 2 s | 1 s | 30 s |

Policy 0 is fixed balanced; policy 2 is the tree with existing rules fallback. The historical JSON also contains `reference_rules`, used here only as a contextual arithmetic check. Yellow/all-red settings are desktop-prototype values, not a claimed public-road safety standard.

The four features are current estimated FIFO queue lengths `(q_NS, q_EW)` and observed arrivals during the past complete 30 seconds `(A_NS, A_EW)`. They are neither future arrivals nor road queue sensor readings. External arrival input is restricted to 0 or 1 per direction per second. Arrivals are inserted into the FIFO before any service in that second. A green direction serves at most one vehicle per second. Same-second arrival and departure can have zero waiting time. Queue-vehicle-seconds (QVS) accumulates both residual queues after arrivals and service.

The header declares queue domain `0..120`, past-30-second arrival count `0..30`, four features, 513 nodes, and maximum depth 10. Archived comparison records report model storage 4,104 bytes and engine state 2,264 bytes. These sizes were not freshly compiled or measured; the node-array source and engine header are absent. Treat the tree as a compact embedded model, without a claim of measured runtime, power, or area advantage.

The original design says CART was trained on labels generated by the existing plan-scoring rule. Training data, training program, hyperparameter record, and model-body source are not included; this lineage is documented, not independently retrained here.

The low-level `ta_model_predict()` header says out-of-domain input returns balanced plan 0. The **actual recovered engine policy** first calls `ta_model_in_domain()` and, outside the domain, retains the rule-selected plan and increments a fallback flag/count. These two behaviors must not be conflated. Invalid model output above plan 2 also falls back to the rule.

Every decision in the recovered engine first evaluates the rule costs for all three plans, even for the fixed/tree policies. Therefore the current source does not establish a firmware speedup from replacing rules with tree inference. It cannot support a claim that tree deployment removed the rule computation.

## Rule formula translated exactly from recovered C

Let `A_d` be the observed count for direction `d` in the previous 30 seconds. For candidate plan `p`, initialize a queue scaled by 30:

`X[d,0] = 30 q_d`.

For each second `t = 1..30`, let `g[d,p,t]` be 1 if direction `d` is green and 0 otherwise:

`X[d,t] = max(0, X[d,t-1] + A_d - 30 g[d,p,t])`.

The integer cost is

`C_p = sum_t (X[NS,t] + X[EW,t])`.

`C_p / 30` is the fluid-lookahead QVS estimate. Thus the estimated constant arrival rate is `A_d / 30` vehicles per second. The reference rule selects the smallest-cost candidate; strict `<` comparisons retain the lower plan ID on a tie. This is a simplified 30-second prediction using past counts, not a guaranteed optimum for the realized next 30 arrivals. The tree's chosen plan is also not guaranteed to minimize these three costs.

## Timing and confirmation boundaries

The source requires a coherent one-second FPGA progression, sufficient history, and last all-red phase (`phase == 5`, `remaining == 1`) before producing a decision. Duplicate snapshots do not consume another observation; skipped ticks and incoherent transitions raise error flags. A plan cannot change within a cycle in the accepted transition logic. At a complete-cycle boundary, a legal new plan can become active without restarting the previous phase countdown.

After input rows `0..599`, 600 observations and 20 decisions exist, but the final decision has no effect on the completed 600-observation performance window. The 54-record historical validator compares 19 applied decisions per case and checks the unused final decision separately (54 × 19 = 1,026 applied comparisons; 54 final-decision checks). Six-pair summary records likewise contain `applied_confirmations: 19` for each of 12 cases. The archived experiment report describes waiting for the twentieth application outside the frozen performance window. Do not state that 20 applications occurred within the 600-observation performance window, or describe the six-pair summary JSON alone as evidence of the twentieth confirmation.

## Hashes and reproducibility limits

All seven snapshots in `source_manifest.json` match their archived SHA-256 and byte length. The current `traffic_agent.c` snapshot also matches the C-source hash named in the historical firmware validation, and `traffic_model.h` matches the corresponding header hash. Model identifier `6c3ac987313535765d2fbdaf8deb1372b8ff22e137c9803e927801a246256262` agrees between header and historical JSON.

The model identifier is distinct from the `traffic_model.c` source-file hash. The actual model-body file is absent, so the model identifier itself cannot be independently recomputed from the model artifact. Similarly, the archived input CSV hash and 1,200-byte board-array hash/CRC cannot be rechecked because those inputs are absent. Hash agreement with an archived manifest supports snapshot identity; it does not independently prove that an earlier execution occurred.

Missing for original experiment rerun: model C source/node arrays, engine header, original arrival CSV, simulator/runner/build sources, RTL/bitstream/platform files, board traces, and board validation/manifests. No original-model inference comparison was attempted because the recovered package lacks the model body; fabricating a replacement would not validate the original model.

## Suggested paper framing

An honest DIC4 contribution can be an evidence-traceable ARM–FPGA traffic-control prototype and its draft specification workflow, with descriptive archival results and a newly reproducible evidence audit. Present task-level integration as design work still pending. The available materials do not support claims of a novel superior traffic-learning algorithm, full agent integration, real-road improvement, deployment safety certification, FPGA fault coverage, measured execution-time/power/area improvement, or complete experimental reproducibility.

Suggested evidence sentence: “We independently recalculated the aggregate metrics and consistency checks from archived validation summaries. No new firmware or physical-board experiment was performed for this draft. The board experiment is reported from its archived experiment report; the raw captures are not part of the recovered package.”
